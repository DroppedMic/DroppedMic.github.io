# Dedication

For the eleven who walked into an empty building at 360 East 145th Street
in September of 2003 and decided it was going to be a school.

For every child who has ever been told the zip code on their birth
certificate was a prophecy.

And for my father, who did not live to see any of this.

# Author's Note

Everything in this book happened. Not all of it happened exactly the way
I tell it.

I have been a principal, a coach, a teacher of principals, and a student
of principals for more than two decades. In that time I have sat with
thousands of children and hundreds of leaders, in buildings from the
South Bronx to Cape Town. When a story in these pages comes from my own
building, MS/HS 223, I have changed names, grades, and identifying
details to protect the privacy of children who were not asked whether
they wanted to be in a book. When a story comes from a principal I
coached, I have done the same, and in several places I have combined
two or three leaders into a single composite so that no one reader
could say, with certainty, that is me. When a composite appears, you
will see a small note in the margin. Nothing in this book is fabricated.
Much of it is softened, rearranged, or disguised, because that is what
it means to write honestly about other people's children and other
people's careers.

The research cited here is real and current as of this writing. Any
misreading of the research belongs to me and not to the scholars I lean
on.

Ramón M. González
Boston and the South Bronx
Spring 2026

# A Note on How to Read This Book

This is not a textbook. It is a field book.

Every framework in these pages has been tested in a building. My
building, at 360 East 145th Street, for eighteen years. And in the
buildings of the principals I have coached, from Mott Haven to Manama.
The theory is here because it earned its place. The stories are here
because they are the theory.

You can read the book in order, which is how I wrote it. Or you can
open it to the chapter that names the week you are having right now,
which is how I hope you use it. Part One is the honest description of
the job nobody trained us for. Part Two is the toolkit, six coaching
traditions that have shaped what I do on a Tuesday afternoon at three
forty-five. Part Three is what the research says about whether any of
it works. Part Four is what to do on Monday if you run a district and
you want to stop losing good principals to exhaustion.

At the end of each chapter you will find a box called *Use Case*. Those
are the true stories, in the disguised form the Author's Note
describes. Read them slowly. They are the reason the framework lived
long enough to make it into this book.

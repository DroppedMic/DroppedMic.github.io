# Coaching the Lead Learner

## A Principal's Field Book for Building Leaders Who Build Schools

**By Dr. Ramón M. González**
*Founding Principal, The Laboratory School of Finance and Technology (MS/HS 223), South Bronx*

---

## A Note on How to Read This Book

This is not a textbook. It is a field book. Every framework in these pages
has been tested in a building — my building, at 360 East 145th Street, and
in the buildings of the principals I have coached from Mott Haven to Manama.
The theory is here because it earned its place. The stories are here because
they are the theory.

---

## Prologue — The Tenement and the Boardroom

*A boy from an East Harlem tenement — father a Vietnam vet, heroin, prison,
AIDS — ends up with a scholarship to Cornell, a doctorate from Teachers
College, and a seat at Columbia Business School teaching executives how to
coach. This book lives in the distance between those two rooms, and argues
that the distance is shorter than most people think.*

- Why I wrote this book
- Who this book is for: the principal at 6:47 a.m. in the parking lot
- A map of the journey ahead

**Anecdote anchor:** The morning I unlocked MS 223 for the first time in
September 2003, and the eleven people who helped me build it.

## Part One — The Work Has Changed. The Preparation Has Not.

### Chapter 1. Mayor of a Six-Block Republic
*Why the principalship is no longer an administrative job.*

- From operations manager to instructional leader to transformational leader
- The three jobs we are now asked to do at once
- Why "professional development" stopped working around 2005

**Use case — "The Monday Morning Problem":** A new principal in Yonkers
inherits a school where three things are on fire before 8 a.m. I walk her
through what coaching — not training — would have prepared her to do.

### Chapter 2. Alone on the Second Floor
*The loneliness epidemic in the principal's office.*

- Isolation as an occupational hazard
- Why superintendents are even lonelier than principals
- The safety-net argument for coaching

**Use case — "The Bathroom Cry":** A first-year principal I coached in
Denver who locked herself in a stall between periods four and five. What
we did about it, and what her district should have done first.

### Chapter 3. What Our Kids Deserve From Us
*Equity as the non-negotiable starting line, not the finish line.*

- 90 percent free lunch, 15 percent in shelters, 20 percent learning
  English — and still above the city average
- Why "every day they walk into this building, they are taking a stand
  against poverty" is not a slogan
- The principal as the guarantor of opportunity

**Use case — "The Bodega Library":** How the Community Reading Initiative
started with one shopkeeper on Willis Avenue and turned into a district
literacy strategy.

## Part Two — Six Coaching Traditions, One Craft

### Chapter 4. Blended Coaching — Gary Bloom, Jackie Wilson, and the End of a False Choice
*Supervision and coaching are not enemies.*

- "Ways of doing" and "ways of being"
- The four stances: facilitative, collaborative, consultative, transformational
- The Adult Dyadic Learning Process, in plain English
- Why the Delaware Academy for School Leadership built its entire
  pipeline around this model

**Use case — "The Assistant Principal Who Would Not Give Feedback":**
I use all four Blended stances in one forty-five-minute cycle. The tape
runs long. You'll see why.

### Chapter 5. Cognitive Coaching — Art Costa, Bob Garmston, and the Voice Inside the Principal's Head
*Coaching that never tells, never judges, and still changes you.*

- The Five States of Mind: Efficacy, Flexibility, Consciousness,
  Craftsmanship, Interdependence
- Pausing, paraphrasing, and the mediative question
- Why I used the States of Mind as my hiring rubric at MS 223

**Use case — "Hiring Ms. Rivera":** The interview where I stopped asking
about lesson plans and started asking about self-talk. She stayed twelve
years.

### Chapter 6. The NISL Executive Development Program and the RAND Verdict
*What a randomized controlled trial taught us about dosage and proximity.*

- The 24-day curriculum and the 60-hour coaching minimum
- The i3 study versus the SEED study: why one worked and one did not
- The decisive variable nobody talks about: where the coach lives

**Use case — "The Out-of-State Coach":** A principal in Pennsylvania was
assigned a coach who had never set foot in the Rust Belt. Three years
later the numbers told the truth.

### Chapter 7. The ISAL Cohort — When Superintendents Find Their People
*Illinois, Cape Town, and the case for a circle you can tell the truth to.*

- The two-year cohort as antidote to isolation
- Near-peer coaches who have sat in your chair
- The personal growth plan and the district plan as twin artifacts

**Use case — "The Cape Town Visit":** Six superintendents, one
eight-hour flight, and the conversation about budget cuts that I could
not have had with anyone in my own district.

### Chapter 8. Emotional Intelligence and Situational Leadership — Reading the Room, Then Reading Yourself
*Goleman meets Hersey-Blanchard in the principal's conference room.*

- Telling, selling, participating, delegating — and when to switch
- The four EI domains as the principal's operating system
- Kolb's learning cycle: how a rough Tuesday becomes durable practice

**Use case — "The Veteran Teacher and the Rookie":** Same feedback
conversation, two different flex styles, two different outcomes.
What I almost got wrong.

### Chapter 9. The NYC Leadership Academy and the LPPW — A Common Language for a Fragmented Profession
*Facilitative coaching with a competency spine.*

- The Leadership Performance Planning Worksheet: nine dimensions,
  low-inference evidence
- Why a shared instrument reduces the variance that plagues principal
  evaluation
- Coaching new principals vs. coaching experienced ones — and why
  the mistake is treating them the same

**Use case — "Chancellor Fariña's First Visit":** She walked my
hallways looking for evidence, not opinion. The LPPW is how I had
trained my team to prepare.

## Part Three — The Leader, The School, The System

### Chapter 10. Coaching and the Burnout Ledger
*What the research says. What I saw. What we owe each other.*

- The 2025 scoping review: 15 countries, 7,369 participants, moderate
  positive effects on wellbeing, turnover, and classroom quality
- The 19.5 percent reduction in emotional exhaustion at five months
- Why wellness is not the same as coaching, and neither is therapy

**Use case — "The Principal Who Almost Quit in October":** She
finished the year, then the next one, and is now a superintendent.
The first twenty minutes of our first call saved the career.

### Chapter 11. Coaching for Equity, or Don't Bother

- The Wallace Foundation's 2023 framework for culturally responsive

- Equity audits as coaching artifacts
- The critical-incident case study as monthly coaching practice

### Chapter 12. From One Principal to a District — Networked Improvement Communities and PLCs

- The PDSA cycle as the coach's second home
- Collective Teacher Efficacy (Hattie d = 1.57) as the downstream

**Use case — "The Three-Principal PDSA":** Three middle school

### Chapter 13. Student Achievement — The Long Chain, Honestly Traced

- What to measure, what to ignore, and what to stop pretending about

**Use case — "The 2010 Intel Award":** The math department, the
coaching cycle that preceded it, and the eighth-grader who stood next

## About the Author

Dr. Ramón M. González is the founding principal of MS/HS 223, The
Laboratory School of Finance and Technology, in the Mott Haven section of
the South Bronx (2003–2021). He is a Professor of Practice at Boston
University's Wheelock College of Education & Human Development, a Cahn
Fellow (2007), and the founder of an executive coaching program for
school leaders in partnership with Columbia Business School and Teachers
College, Columbia University. He has coached, taught, and spoken about
school leadership in Boston, New York, Denver, Yonkers, The Hague, Cape
Town, Manama, Rio de Janeiro, Bangkok, and Madrid. He was recognized as
a White House Champion of Change for Arts Education by President Barack
Obama, and his work has been profiled by the *New York Times Magazine*,
*Smithsonian*, the *Wall Street Journal*, and PBS. He grew up in an East
Harlem tenement. He holds a BA from Cornell, an MS from City College,
and an MA and Ed.M from Teachers College, Columbia University.
